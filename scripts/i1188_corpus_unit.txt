The morning market opened under a wide awning, and the first customers arrived before the vendors had finished arranging their stalls. A baker set out loaves of dark bread, while a farmer unloaded crates of apples and pears from a wooden cart.
By noon the square was crowded. Children chased each other between the tables, and an old musician played a tune on a battered fiddle. The noise of haggling rose and fell like the tide.
An innkeeper watched the crowd from her doorway, counting the strangers who might need a room for the night. Two travelers with dusty boots asked the price of soup, then sat down near the window and shared a single bowl between them.
When the afternoon light turned gold, the vendors began to pack their goods. The baker had sold every loaf. The farmer had three crates of apples left, and gave one to the children who had been running all day.
The musician played until the square was empty, then folded his fiddle under his arm and walked home through the quiet streets, humming the last tune to himself.
