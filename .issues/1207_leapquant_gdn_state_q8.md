## Why

The hybrid lane quantizes the KV half (`ternary_kvq.rs`, Issue 919 T3) and defaults every GDN recurrent state to f32 — the sole exception being the opt-in per-step f16 `RIIR_GDN_STATE_HALF` named below — while the league's losing axis is decode (m5ultra re-cell 0.888×/0.863×/0.842×, Bench 1161; 4090 tg128 ~0.970×), and the state read+write is per-token bandwidth both boxes pay. The lane's own named structural successor was already state precision: `RIIR_GDN_STATE_HALF` (riir-ai Plan 611 / Bench 954) shipped per-step f16/bf16 — banked +0.44%, resident 144→72 MiB — and its promotion was DECLINED on the measured blocker: sequence-level forking at deployment lengths. LeapQuant (published, training-free) is the accuracy-first retake of the same lever: windowed INT8 states at FP32-level accuracy on the Qwen/GLM/Kimi hybrid families our lanes serve, at ¼ the bytes. Two live consumers: the decode campaign (an armed lane awaiting an accuracy-safe arm) and Issue 1201's serving checkpoints (3.4× smaller states → the paper's −41–56% memory class).

## Constraints

- State layout law is ours: the reference implementation's slot-aliasing trick (8-bit checkpoint packed inside vLLM's f32 state tensor) is vLLM-shape-specific; our pools own their layout.
- Paper defaults ride as constants until a cell justifies otherwise: p=16 window, r=4 FP16 CTs (r=6 at 6/4-bit), FP32 smoothing scales, smoothing recomputed every boundary.
- The paper's speedups are CUDA-only numbers; the Metal arm's expected gain rests on the bandwidth argument alone — T3's Metal row is exploratory until measured, never quoted as a paper-confirmed number.
- The accuracy evidence is downstream-benchmark-level (no perplexity tables in the main text) — our G1 is the 879-harness plateau/impulse arms + the argmax-flip census, never a quoted accuracy transfer.
- Model scope: qwen3.8 (GDN) first; Bonsai-2-27B (ternary GDN, bonsai2_hadamard-rotated states quantize post-rotation); GLM/KDA lane is NOT in scope here (no KDA lane exists in this repo — a future lane rides this issue's machinery).
