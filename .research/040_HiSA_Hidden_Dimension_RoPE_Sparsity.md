## TL;DR

HiSA makes attention sparse in the **hidden (feature) dimension** instead of over tokens: RoPE decomposes each query–key score into P = d/2 per-frequency-pair parts, and HiSA keeps part *l* only while its rotation `Δ·θ_l < Φ` (angle budget, default 90°) — a **static, parameter-free, position-only** rule with closed-form per-pair windows `Δ_max,l = Φ·b^(2l/d)` and reach `R = Φ·b^((d−2)/d)`. Trained from scratch it *beats* dense loss at ~30% of the QK compute; a block rule lands in FlashAttention-3 at dense speed; per-chunk KV ring buffers + value eviction beyond the reach shrink the cache ~33% and speed decoding +42% @16K/batch-128 — **in the paper's b=1e4/d=64 regime**. §5 measures that dense models learn to *suppress* high-rotation score parts (87–95% of cells beyond 180° suppressed in SmolLM-135M).

**Why it matters here — and why it mostly doesn't (the honest regime statement):** BOTH league backbones were header-probed this session and BOTH are **partial-RoPE qwen35-arch hybrids** — Bonsai-2-27B and the qwen3.8-27B backbone each rotate only `rope.dimension_count = 64` of their 256 key dims (32 of 128 pairs; sections `[11, 11, 10, 0]`; `full_attention_interval = 4`). The 96 unrotated pairs never rotate ⇒ HiSA always keeps them, so the decode lever collapses to **10.0% @2K / 13.6% @16K / 14.5% @32K** at Φ=90° (5–11% at the training-free 720° posture) — marginal against the Issue-1211 ≥5%-per-shape e2e bar in single-stream decode, on 1/4 of layers. The big-base stretch also means value eviction NEVER fires (R ≈ 9.5M over the rotated dims ≫ 262k). The paper's cache-memory and +42%-decode wins are b=1e4/full-rotary-regime effects that do not transfer to anything we serve. What survives: (a) the **architecture fact** that 75% of every attention key cache is ALREADY position-free — the model class already made half of HiSA's trade; (b) a cheap modelless **suppression census** (now tiny: 16–17 attention layers × 32 rotating pairs) that decides whether the remaining 25% shows distance-conditional suppression worth anything; and (c) the **planner as a guard** — its first live catch was this note's own headline (the review's, §7). The one full-rotary surface in the league is the 5-block DFlash2 drafter (d=128, 64 pairs, no dimension_count key) — HiSA there would be a 39–57% cut of the DRAFTER's QK work, but the specdec lanes are PAUSED (verdict d9c2bb28), so it is recorded, never filed. Planner arithmetic verified against the paper's own Table 8 (exact-window closed form: 23.86% vs their 23.9% at their regime).

## 1. Paper Core Findings

1. **Exact decomposition.** With RoPE, `s_ij = (1/√d) Σ_l q_i^(l)·k_j^(l)·cos(φ_ij^(l) + Δ·θ_l)`, Δ = i−j, θ_l = b^(−2l/d). Content enters through norms + φ; position only through Δθ_l. Fast pairs (small l) rotate ~1 rad per token — their score part is pure positional noise at distance; slow pairs still capture long-range structure.
2. **The rule.** Keep part l iff `Δ·θ_l < Φ`. No parameters, no router, no top-k; the pattern is known before any token is seen (static), so kernels skip work without a selection pass. Φ=90° default (quarter period); every finite budget 45°–360° beat dense in ablation.
3. **Training results (from scratch, SmolLM-135M/360M, b=1e4/2e4, d=64, 4K ctx).** HiSA reaches LOWER val loss than dense (2.880 vs 2.906 @135M) at 29–34% of QK compute. Exact Triton rule trains 1.7× slower than dense; the FA3 **block rule** (S=128 key blocks × 16-feature chunks = 8 pairs, boundary chunk zeroed in Q) trains at dense FA3 speed keeping ~half the loss gain. 16K extension (540M tok, b=2e4): advantage held at 20% compute.
4. **KV cache + decode.** Per-pair windows → per-chunk key ring buffers (ring = window of the chunk's slowest pair) + value eviction beyond reach R. Cache ~33% smaller; decode +42% tok/s @16K/batch-128 (13% of it from smaller query tiles). ρ counts QK multiply-adds; softmax/V work only shrink for keys beyond reach — which is why the saving appears in decode (cache-read dominated) first. ⚠ ALL of these figures are the paper's b=1e4, FULL-rotary (d=64, all 32 pairs rotate) regime — see §2 for what survives at our partial-RoPE big-base geometry.
5. **Conversion.** Dense → HiSA with NO training: 2.937 vs 2.906 (worse by ~1.1% relative) at 29% compute. With 1.35B continued tokens (10% of pretraining budget): 2.836 vs 2.838 dense-continued — parity at 29% compute.
6. **Interpretability (§5) — the modelless payload.** Per (layer, pair, distance-band): compare the head-output effect of part *l* with the real key (`E_real`) vs an **uninformative null** — the same pair computed with an unrelated key at the same position (same rotation, unrelated content; the null key is the key at the same position in the next document of the batch). `E_real/E_null < 1` = the model actively suppresses that part. At init all cells ≈ 1; small angles turn red (used) fast; beyond the 90° line cells turn blue slowly — 87–95% of fast-pair cells beyond 180° suppressed in SmolLM-135M (~600B tok), suppression still progressing at end of the paper's own runs. A scale-normalized variant (÷√(2n_ij)) shows the loss gain is NOT the implicit down-weighting of distant scores (2.879 ≈ HiSA 2.880 ≪ dense 2.906).

## 5. Published prior-art landscape (searches cited, novelty waived)

- **ATFlash** (Hayashi et al., arXiv:2608.02947): per-RoPE-wavelength windows applied to PRETRAINED models without training (two periods default), FA4 prefill + FlashInfer decode; skips compute + key reads but no KV-cache reduction. This owns the training-free window lane — our training-free posture must quote it, not claim it.
- **WavePrune** (Du et al., arXiv:2610.06963): one rotation period per pair, training-free AND from-scratch GPT-2 with FA forward/backward kernels; explains the window by aliasing in pretrained attention maps. No KV reduction. Its aliasing law doubles as a closed-form per-pair PRIOR the census can check itself against (predicted-vs-measured suppression agreement = an instrument-calibration check).
- **In-paper related work** (verified in full text): SparQ/Loki/DoubleSparsity/FASA — hidden dims as a TOKEN-SELECTION proxy (index few dims, then full product on selected keys); ThinK — content-pruned key-cache channels, same pruning for all but recent tokens; Sparse Feature Attention (arXiv:2603.22300) — content-chosen top-k features per vector, trained; NSA/MoBA/Quest/H2O/StreamingLLM — the token-sparse family. HiSA's differentiators: position-only static rule, trains with tight budgets, KV reduction, and the §5 suppression measurement.
- **Hong et al. 2024** "On the token distance modeling ability of higher RoPE": earlier dimension-level RoPE-frequency analysis (surfaced in this session's web sweep).
- **Internal corpus:** katgpt-rs 086 RTPurbo is the complementary finding — "long-range retrieval is governed by LOW-frequency RoPE components; 16-dim pre-RoPE projector >90% recall". Consistent with HiSA (fast pairs are the positional-noise channel at distance; slow pairs carry content). RTPurbo is a proxy (hidden dims SELECT keys); HiSA is the final sparsity (no second stage). The signal-diff is clean; no coverage conflict. Research 020's saturation law + league-shape crossover (≤32k) is the standing frame this note plans inside.

## 7. Honest caveats

- **The controlling caveat is this note's own history:** the first draft computed its headline numbers (~40–52% decode cut) from a full-rotary reading of `attention.key_length` and skipped `rope.dimension_count` — the header key that shows BOTH league backbones rotate only 64/256 dims. The verdict reviewer caught it by probing the real headers; this revision re-derived everything from them (10–14.5% class, tier demoted to RECORD in the same landing). The planner (`.issues/1244` T1) exists to make this error class impossible: `rope.dimension_count`, `rope.dimension_sections`, `full_attention_interval`, and `block_count` are REQUIRED reads before any window arithmetic.
- **27B transfer is an extrapolation.** The paper's models are 135M–360M at b=1e4; our checkpoints are 27B-class at b=1e7 with ~1000× stretched windows AND partial rotary. The census measures OUR models directly — it de-risks, never eliminates.
